## The question

Safety evaluation asks whether model behavior is harmful or robust; mechanistic interpretability asks what internal components explain that behavior. Connecting behavioral and mechanistic evidence is valuable but not automatic.

## Approach

For EE269 at UC Riverside, I prepared a paper-based presentation spanning four recent works in LLM safety, mechanistic interpretability, and their intersection. The goal was to organize claims, methods, and caveats into a readable comparative account for a graduate audience. Slides and source material are linked in the [presentation repository](https://github.com/Vedant2100/EE269-Presentation).

## Evidence and results

This is a literature synthesis rather than a new empirical benchmark. Its contribution is the selection, explanation, and cross-paper comparison; experimental results belong to the respective papers and should be attributed there.

## Limitations

Interpretability findings are often model- and intervention-specific. A visualization, activation probe, or circuit claim is not by itself causal evidence of safety.
